The human DLX5 protein (UniProt ID P56178) has a length of 289 amino acids, and localises 
primarily to the cell nucleus, consistent with its function as a DNA-binding developmental 
regulator.

This practical aimed to compare the evolutionary conservation of the DLX5 homeobox protein 
between Homo sapiens and Mus musculus, using UniProt BLAST and Python, ungapped global alignment 
script with the BLOSUM62 scoring matrix.

Online UniProt BLAST confirmed DLX5 is highly conserved across mammalian species. The pairwise 
alignment results showed that human and mouse DLX5 achieved a BLOSUM62 total score of 1490 and 
a sequence identity of 96.54%, indicating extremely strong structural and functional homology. 
In contrast, alignments between either native DLX5 sequence and a randomly generated protein 
reference yielded strongly negative total scores and extremely low identity (~6.5–7.0%), close 
to the random baseline expectation.

The large discrepancy between native species alignment and random control demonstrates that 
the high similarity is not due to chance. The high conservation of DLX5 across divergent mammals 
reflects its critical, constrained role in embryonic development and gene regulation. 
In conclusion, this exercise verified the use of substitution matrices and sequence alignment to 
infer evolutionary relatedness, and confirmed DLX5 as an deeply conserved essential protein.
