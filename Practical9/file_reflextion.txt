Reflection on the Practical
Overall, this practical was a challenging parts.
The basic SIR model was relatively easy to understand and implement, as the logic was clear and followed standard probabilistic rules. The vaccination extension was fun because it showed real-world meaning like herd immunity.
The most difficult part was the 2D spatial SIR model. Handling grid boundaries, 8-neighbor infection, and updating the population without overwriting values required careful thinking. Debugging issues like negative populations or out-of-bounds errors was challenging but helpful.
I enjoyed the visualization parts the most, especially seeing the disease spread across the 2D grid. It made the model feel concrete and realistic.
The most important thing I learned is how probabilistic models work in both well-mixed and spatial populations, and how small changes in parameters can completely change disease outcomes.
